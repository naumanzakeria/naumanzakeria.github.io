The Habits That Improved My Leadership

Nauman Zakeria · Customer Success, Experience & Service Leader

Nobody hands you leadership skills when you get promoted. I found that out directly — the habits that made me good at managing my own accounts didn't automatically transfer to leading a team of Customer Success Advisors and Support Specialists. I had to build new ones, deliberately, often by getting something wrong first.

I started writing things down before saying them out loud. Early on, I'd walk into a coaching conversation with a general sense of what needed to be said, and it would come out muddled, sometimes landing more harshly than I intended. Now I write the actual point down first — one sentence, no hedging — before the conversation happens. It forced clarity on me before I asked for it from someone else.

I stopped confusing "available" with "helpful." I used to think being a good leader meant being reachable for every question, immediately, always. What I actually built was a team that came to me reflexively instead of thinking it through first. Now I ask a question back before I answer one — "what would you do here?" — more often than I hand over the answer. It's slower in the moment and faster over months.

I started separating the person from the outcome. When something on my team didn't go well, my early instinct was to focus the conversation on what went wrong. I've learned to separate that clearly from who the person is — "this approach didn't get the result we needed" is a completely different conversation than one that feels like a verdict on someone's competence, even if the underlying facts are the same. The first one invites someone to improve. The second one invites someone to get defensive, or quietly disengage.

I learned to sit with silence in one-on-ones. My instinct used to be filling every pause with reassurance or a solution. Some of the most useful things my team has ever told me only came out after I stopped filling that silence and just waited.

None of these habits came naturally. I built every one of them by getting the earlier version wrong first, noticing it, and deciding to do it differently next time. That, more than any single skill, might be the actual definition of leadership growth.
