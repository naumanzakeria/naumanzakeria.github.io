The Habits That Improved My Leadership

by Nauman Zakeria · Customer Success, Experience & Service Leader

Leadership development is often discussed in terms of major philosophies.

My experience has been more practical.

The biggest improvements often came from small habits repeated consistently.

Listen Before Solving

When someone brings a problem, the natural instinct is to provide an answer.

I learned to ask questions first.

Sometimes people need a decision. Sometimes they need coaching. Sometimes they already know the answer and simply need space to think.

Explain the Why

Instructions create compliance. Context creates judgment.

When people understand why something matters, they can make better decisions even when the manager isn't present.

Give Feedback Close to the Moment

Waiting months to discuss something that could have been addressed today helps nobody.

Useful feedback should be timely, specific and focused on behavior.

Recognize Good Work

People should not hear from their manager only when something goes wrong.

Recognition doesn't need to be dramatic.

A specific acknowledgment of good judgment, customer ownership or teamwork can reinforce the behaviors you want repeated.

Reflect on My Own Decisions

Not every poor outcome is an employee problem.

Sometimes the expectation was unclear. Sometimes the process was weak. Sometimes the leader made the wrong call.

Reflection turns experience into learning.

Stay Calm Under Pressure

Customer-facing environments can become intense.

Escalations happen. Targets are missed. Systems fail.

A leader's emotional response affects the room.

Calm does not mean ignoring urgency. It means responding to urgency without creating unnecessary chaos.

Leadership, for me, has become less about having a particular style and more about developing habits that help other people perform at their best.
