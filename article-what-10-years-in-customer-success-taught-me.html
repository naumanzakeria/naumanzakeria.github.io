What 10+ Years in Customer Success Has Taught Me

by Nauman Zakeria · Customer Success, Experience & Service Leader

More than a decade of working across customer-facing roles has changed how I think about Customer Success.

Early in a career, it is easy to believe that great customer service means having the right answer.

Experience teaches you that the answer is only part of it.

Customers Remember Ownership

I have learned that customers can often accept problems.

What frustrates them most is uncertainty: not knowing who owns the issue, what happens next, or whether anyone is listening.

Ownership creates confidence even before the problem is solved.

Listening Is More Valuable Than Talking

The strongest customer conversations are rarely the ones where I speak the most.

Customers tell you what matters if you create enough room to listen.

Sometimes the real issue appears after the obvious issue has been discussed.

Data and Relationships Need Each Other

My experience across Customer Success, customer operations and analytics has reinforced something important for me:

Data makes relationships stronger when used correctly.

Metrics help identify risk, performance changes and opportunities. But numbers without context can mislead.

I want both the dashboard and the conversation.

Leadership Is About Enabling Others

Managing people also changed my understanding of success.

A leader's performance cannot be measured only by what the leader personally delivers.

It is reflected in whether the team becomes more confident, capable and accountable.

Customer Success Is a Commercial Function

Retention protects revenue. Adoption strengthens value. Strong relationships create expansion opportunities. Customer advocacy supports growth.

Customer Success therefore should not sit at the edge of business strategy. It should be part of it.

Relationships Still Matter Most

Tools have changed enormously throughout my career. Customer expectations have changed. Automation has improved. AI is changing the profession again.

But one principle has remained remarkably consistent:

People want to work with people they trust.

That is the lesson I keep coming back to.
