Product Adoption Is the Real Measure of SaaS Success

Nauman Zakeria · Customer Success, Experience & Service Leader

Every SaaS company I've worked with tracks the same vanity metric obsessively: logo count. New customers signed. Deals closed. Nobody throws a party in the all-hands meeting for "our existing customers actually used the thing they bought."

That's backwards, and it's expensive.

I've onboarded 15+ enterprise customers and led full implementation-to-adoption journeys, and here's the pattern I've seen over and over: a signed contract is not a success. It's a hypothesis. The hypothesis is "this customer will get enough value from our product that they'll want to keep paying for it." A signature doesn't test that hypothesis. Usage does.

I've watched companies celebrate a closed enterprise deal, then quietly lose that same customer eleven months later because nobody made sure the product actually got used past week three. The sales team hit their number. The business didn't actually grow — it just borrowed revenue from the future and called it a win in the present.

Adoption is uncomfortable to measure honestly because it forces you to admit when your onboarding is failing, when your UX is confusing, or when the customer bought something that doesn't fit their actual workflow. Logo count never forces that kind of honesty. It just keeps climbing while the back door quietly empties out.

When I directed customer journeys from onboarding through long-term engagement, I paid far more attention to which features customers actually opened in week two than to whether the kickoff call "went well." Kickoff calls always go well. Everyone's polite in week one.

If your SaaS company wants a number that actually predicts revenue six months from now, stop starting with how many deals closed. Start with how many of last quarter's customers are still logging in — and doing the thing the product was bought to do. That number is uncomfortable. It's also the only one that's honest.
