Leadership

Leadership Begins with Trust

Nauman Zakeria · Customer Success, Experience & Service Leader

I've mentored Customer Success Advisors and Support Specialists, and if there's one thing I've learned about leading a team, it's this: people don't follow your title. They follow whether they believe you'll back them when it costs you something.

That belief isn't built in team meetings. It's built in the moments nobody's watching — when a team member makes a judgment call that doesn't go perfectly, and you have a choice: protect them and coach them privately, or throw them under the bus to look good upward. I've been on both sides of that moment, as the person being led and the person leading, and I can tell you which one actually builds a team that performs.

Trust is also built by being the person who tells the truth even when it's inconvenient. Early in my career, in a much more junior seat, I watched a manager tell our team a project was "going great" right up until it collapsed publicly. Nobody trusted a word out of that manager again. I decided then that I'd rather deliver an uncomfortable update early than a comfortable lie that falls apart later.

When you lead a Customer Success or support team, you're asking people to make judgment calls constantly — how firm to be with an angry customer, when to escalate, when to bend a policy. They can only make good calls quickly if they trust that you'll stand behind reasonable judgment, even when it doesn't land perfectly. Without that trust, every decision gets slow and defensive, run past you "just to be safe" — and a team that's afraid to decide is a team that can't scale.

Leadership isn't the authority to tell people what to do. It's having earned enough trust that they want your opinion before you give it.
