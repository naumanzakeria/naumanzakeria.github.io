Creating Accountability Without Micromanagement

Nauman Zakeria · Customer Success, Experience & Service Leader

There's a particular kind of manager who confuses "watching everything" with "leading well." I've worked for a few. What I noticed is that the tighter they gripped, the less their teams actually grew — because nobody develops judgment when someone else is always making the final call.

Real accountability isn't built by checking someone's work constantly. It's built by being extremely clear about the outcome you need, genuinely clear about why it matters, and then getting out of the way long enough for them to own it — mistakes included.

When I coach Customer Success Advisors, I don't hand them a script for every customer conversation. I give them the outcome: the customer needs to leave this interaction feeling heard and with an actual resolution path, not just a "your ticket has been escalated" brush-off. How they get there — tone, pacing, which questions they ask first — that's theirs to develop. If I dictate every word, I haven't built a Customer Success Advisor. I've built a person who can only repeat what I told them, which falls apart the second a situation isn't the one I scripted for.

Accountability also requires that consequences (positive and corrective) are actually tied to outcomes, not to whether someone did things exactly the way you would have. I've had team members solve a customer issue differently than I would have — and better. If I'd been micromanaging, I'd have prevented the better solution from ever surfacing.

The uncomfortable part of this approach is that it requires tolerating some mistakes you could have prevented by controlling everything. I've made that trade deliberately, every time, because a team that's allowed to occasionally get it wrong while learning will always outperform a team that's only ever allowed to get it exactly the way you dictated.

Micromanagement feels like control. It's actually a ceiling.
