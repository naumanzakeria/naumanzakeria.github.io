Customer Experience

Customer Experience Is Every Interaction

Nauman Zakeria · Customer Success, Experience & Service Leader

Companies love to talk about "the customer experience" like it's a single owned initiative — a redesigned onboarding flow, a new support portal, a rebrand. I understand the appeal of treating it that way: it's easier to budget for a project than to admit that experience is actually happening in hundreds of uncoordinated moments you don't fully control.

Here's the truth from a decade of sitting inside those moments: the customer experience isn't the flagship project. It's the hold music. It's whether the second person they talk to already knows what the first person told them. It's the tone of a single automated email sent at 11pm when their invoice fails. Customers don't experience your company as a strategy document. They experience it as a sequence of specific, forgettable-to-you, unforgettable-to-them interactions.

I've supported customers across phone, email, and chat, and I've watched the same account form an opinion of an entire company based on one five-minute call. Not the product. Not the pricing. One call. That's not fair, and it's also just how people actually work — we generalize from our most recent and most emotional interaction, every time.

This means customer experience can't be delegated entirely to a "CX team" and considered handled. Every department that ever touches a customer — billing, product, support, sales, even the automated system that sends a password reset email — is quietly either building trust or spending it down. Most companies only actively manage the interactions they've labeled "customer-facing," while the accidental ones — a confusing invoice, an unclear error message — do damage nobody's tracking.

If I could change one thing about how companies approach experience, it's this: stop treating it as an initiative with a launch date. Start treating it as a standard every single interaction is held to, on purpose, all the time — because the customer isn't grading you on the project. They're grading you on the last five minutes they spent with you, whenever that was, wherever it happened.
