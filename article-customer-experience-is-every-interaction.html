Customer Experience

Customer Experience Is Every Interaction

by Nauman Zakeria · Customer Success, Experience & Service Leader

Customer Experience is often treated as a department.

Customers don't experience departments. They experience moments.

The website they visit. The salesperson who responds. The onboarding process. The invoice. The product. The support conversation. The renewal discussion.

Together, those moments become the brand.

Small Friction Accumulates

A confusing email may seem minor. So might a delayed response. Or asking a customer to repeat information they already provided.

Individually, each issue may be small. Collectively, they communicate something much bigger:

"Doing business with us is difficult."

Map the Journey From the Customer's Perspective

Internal processes are often designed around organizational convenience.

Journey mapping forces us to ask different questions.

What is the customer trying to accomplish at this stage? What do they need? Where are they waiting? Where are they confused? Where are we asking them to do unnecessary work?

Consistency Builds Confidence

A great sales experience followed by poor onboarding is not a great customer experience.

Neither is an excellent product supported by frustrating service.

Customers judge the whole relationship. That is why CX must be cross-functional.

Every Employee Influences Experience

Customer Experience is not owned solely by CX teams.

Product influences it. Finance influences it. Operations influences it. Technology influences it. Leadership influences it.

The role of CX leadership is therefore not to "own" every interaction. It is to help the organization understand how those interactions connect.

Because the customer doesn't see your departments. They see your company.
